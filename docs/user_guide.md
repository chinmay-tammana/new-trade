# Trader User Guide: Goldman Sachs (GS) Directional Trading Indicator

This guide instructs discretionary and systematic paper traders on how to interpret, monitor, and safely utilize the predictions generated by the GS AI trading indicator model (`model-v1`).

---

## 1. Indicator Outputs and Signal Interpretation

At the conclusion of each hourly trading candle, the model ingests market bar data and emits three primary fields:

### 1.1 Predicted Probability ($\hat{p}_t$)
- $\hat{p}_t \in [0.0, 1.0]$ represents the **calibrated posterior probability** that the subsequent 1-hour return of `GS` will exceed $+10\text{ bps}$ ($+0.0010$).
- Because the model is calibrated using Platt scaling, $\hat{p}_t$ reflects real empirical frequency: if the model assigns $\hat{p} = 0.58$, approximately 58 out of 100 historical instances with similar feature distributions exhibited an upward move.

### 1.2 Directional Action Signal ($s_t$)
The operational decision rule deployed in `model-v1` utilizes an asymmetric margin threshold:
- **Up Signal ($s_t = +1$)**: Emitted when $\hat{p}_t \ge 0.56$ (Up margin $d_{\text{up}} = 0.06$).
  - *Trader Interpretation*: The model identifies a statistically favorable upward bias for the next 60 minutes.
  - *Paper Action*: Simulated long entry with a target horizon of 1 hour, or an upward bias filter for discretionary intraday execution.
- **Down Signal ($s_t = -1$)**: Currently **DISABLED** in `model-v1` ($d_{\text{down}} = \text{None}$).
  - *Trader Interpretation*: The model will **never** emit a Down signal under the frozen v1 configuration. Downside predictions exhibited poor out-of-fold calibration during hyperparameter search, and the risk engine elected to disable shorting rather than incur false short losses.
- **No Signal / Abstention ($s_t = 0$)**: Emitted when $\hat{p}_t < 0.56$.
  - *Trader Interpretation*: Market conditions are ambiguous, noisy, or balanced.
  - *Paper Action*: Maintain cash or flat positioning. Do not force trades when the indicator abstains. Abstention is a core capital preservation mechanism (coverage is approximately 21%).

## 2. Execution Timing and Candle Mechanics

### 2.1 Candle Schedule (Regular Trading Hours, Eastern Time)
The system operates on seven regular-hours session candles:
1. **Candle 0 (09:30 – 10:30)**: First morning hour. High opening volatility.
2. **Candle 1 (10:30 – 11:29)**: Late morning session.
3. **Candle 2 (11:30 – 12:29)**: Early midday session.
4. **Candle 3 (12:30 – 13:29)**: Lunch session (low volume, chop risk).
5. **Candle 4 (13:30 – 14:29)**: Early afternoon session.
6. **Candle 5 (14:30 – 15:29)**: Late afternoon session.
7. **Candle 6 (15:30 – 16:00)**: Final session candle (30 minutes). **Signals are strictly censored ($s_t = 0$).**

### 2.2 Order Timing
- The signal is generated at the close of candle $t$ (e.g., at 11:30:00).
- If $s_t = +1$, simulated entry occurs at the Open of candle $t+1$ (e.g., 11:30:00).
- The trade is held for exactly 1 hour and liquidated at the Close of candle $t+1$ (e.g., 12:29:59).

## 3. Critical Circuit Breakers: When NOT to Trust the Indicator

Trading models trained on technical and benchmark momentum are fragile to macroeconomic and corporate events. **Traders must manually override and halt the indicator under the following conditions:**

### 3.1 Scheduled Macroeconomic Releases (FOMC, CPI, PPI, NFP)
- **Rule**: Halt all trading 30 minutes before and 60 minutes after scheduled major economic announcements.
- **Rationale**: High-frequency interest rate repricing overrides historical technical patterns, resulting in violent whipsaws.

### 3.2 Goldman Sachs Earnings Announcements
- **Rule**: No signals may be executed on the day of GS quarterly earnings release, or the trading day preceding earnings.
- **Rationale**: Idiosyncratic earnings gap risk is unpredictable by intraday technical momentum models.

### 3.3 The Opening Candle (Candle 0: 09:30 – 10:30)
- **Rule**: Exercise extreme skepticism if an Up signal is emitted at 10:30 following a large overnight opening gap.
- **Rationale**: Error analysis showed high false-positive rates on opening gap fades where early momentum failed to hold.

### 3.4 The Final Candle (Candle 6: 15:30 – 16:00)
- **Rule**: **NEVER hold overnight.**
- **Rationale**: The indicator is trained exclusively on intraday price action. Holding overnight exposes capital to 17.5 hours of unmodeled news risk and earnings reports. All intraday positions must be flat by 16:00 ET.

### 3.5 Half-Days and Abbreviated Sessions
- **Rule**: Disable the indicator completely during early-market-close sessions (e.g., Black Friday, day before Christmas/July 4th).
- **Rationale**: Session-anchored candle structures break down on half-days.

### 3.6 Bear Market Regimes (Under 50-Day Moving Average)
- **Rule**: If `GS` is trading below its daily 50-day simple moving average (SMA), disable all Up signals.
- **Rationale**: `model-v1` is an Up-only indicator. During sustained macro sell-offs, buying every intraday bounce leads to severe drawdowns (as demonstrated on the 2025 held-out test block).

## 4. Live Data Requirements: 1-Minute Microstructure & Volume Feeds

1. **Live 1-Minute Bar Feed Requirement**:
   The V2 feature matrix requires live 1-minute OHLCV bars inside each hour to compute the five microstructure features:
   - `realized_vol_1m` (intrabar standard deviation of 1-minute returns)
   - `up_minute_ratio` (fraction of green 1-minute candles)
   - `clv` (Close Location Value)
   - `signed_volume_ratio` (order volume directionality)
   - `last15_ret` (price momentum over final 15 minutes of the hour)
   A live trading backend must buffer 1-minute bars during the hour and aggregate them at minute 59.

2. **IEX vs. Consolidated SIP Volume**:
   - The indicator is trained on the consolidated **SIP feed** (capturing CTA/UTP tapes across all US exchanges: NYSE, Nasdaq, Cboe, ARCA, IEX).
   - If a live trader executes using a free broker API that defaults to an **IEX-only feed**, observed volume may be 10x to 50x lower than SIP consolidated volume. While ratio features like `signed_volume_ratio` and `up_minute_ratio` normalize volume, `rvol` may exhibit noise if market share on IEX shifts. It is strongly recommended to use a consolidated SIP feed for live paper inference.

## 5. Friction and Capital Management
- **Transaction Costs**: Real-world execution involves bid-ask spread and broker exchange fees. Backtests assume a conservative **5.0 bps ($0.0005$)** friction per round trip. In live paper execution, if the bid-ask spread on `GS` exceeds $0.15 (approx. 3 bps on a \$500 stock), cancel the entry.
- **Position Sizing**: Limit each trade to no more than 1–2% of allocated paper trading capital. Never pyramid or double down on losing candles.
- **Stop-Loss Discipline**: Although the theoretical model evaluates time-based exits (exit after 1 hour), paper traders should maintain a hard stop-loss of -50 bps (-0.50%) to protect against intraday market shocks.

## 5. Summary Checklist Before Executing Any Signal

- [ ] Is the current time between 10:30 and 15:30 ET?
- [ ] Is the current candle NOT the 15:30–16:00 close?
- [ ] Is today free of FOMC, CPI, or GS Earnings releases?
- [ ] Is the bid-ask spread tight ($< 0.15$ USD)?
- [ ] Did the model emit $s_t = +1$ with $\hat{p}_t \ge 0.56$?
- [ ] Are you prepared to exit in exactly 60 minutes or at 16:00 ET, whichever comes first?

If all boxes are checked, the trade complies with the paper trading operating protocol.
